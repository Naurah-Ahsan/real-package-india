How this works

The arithmetic

Every preparation year charges the coaching cost. Every college year charges a quarter of the four-year total. Nothing comes in during either. From graduation the engineer earns the chosen salary basis, rising 8% a year, since early-career salary growth in India runs well ahead of general wage growth.

Payback is the first year total earnings cover total cost. Everything is pre-tax, in nominal rupees, and education loan interest is left out, which would push payback later.

Where the numbers come from
  • What graduates earn — analysis of Periodic Labour Force Survey data: ₹28,000 a month is the median for an employed engineering graduate aged 22–24, and ₹50,000 is the 90th percentile. Of employed engineering graduates, 58% work in software, earning ₹32,600 a month.
  • Employability by branch — India Skills Report 2026 (Wheebox and ETS). Computer science scores 80% and IT 78%; engineering overall is 70.15%, down from 71.5% in 2025.
  • Graduate unemployment — PLFS Annual Report 2025, released by the National Statistical Office in March 2026: 11.2% among graduates against 3.1% nationally.
  • Placement claims — reconciliation of NIRF-ranked colleges' reported placements against the national supply of entry-level jobs at that salary.
  • Fees — published 2026 fee structures. State government colleges run ₹8–13 lakh over four years including hostel; premium private deemed universities exceed ₹30 lakh.
  • Coaching — published 2026 institute fees. Kota or a metro branch with hostel runs ₹3.5–5.5 lakh a year once food, travel and books are counted; online coaching runs ₹40,000–1.5 lakh a year.

Branch employability marked ≈ is estimated inside the published spread rather than taken from a specific figure. The percentile for the advertised package is modelled by fitting a lognormal curve to the published median and 90th percentile, so read it as an estimate of position, not a measured value.

What this leaves out on purpose

Part-time work and internships. Education loan interest. Tax. The enormous gap between an IIT graduate and a tier-three college graduate, which a national median flattens. Scholarships and fee waivers. The fact that people who clear entrance exams differ from those who don't in ways that would show up in their earnings anyway.

It also treats a degree purely as a financial instrument, which is the one thing it certainly is not.

National medians. Your own number will differ, and for a small minority it will differ enormously — which is exactly what an average package hides.
